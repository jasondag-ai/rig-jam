# Rush Hour Rigs: standing rules (read with GAME_BIBLE.md)

These rules override anything older in the Game Bible. Every gag, prop and scene change must pass all of them. Added Oct 6, 2026.

## 1. Depth rule (Jay, Oct 6)
Every scene has one believable front-to-back order, and every animation honours it.
- Side-view strips use the ground line for depth: whatever stands lower on screen (feet or base closer to the viewer) draws in front; whatever stands higher (further back) draws behind. This applies to characters, animals, props and scenery alike (cow, bull, bushes, trees, biffy, lease sign, riser, bale, puddles, mounds).
- Each gag character walks on a fixed lane (a ground line). A character passes a prop either clearly in front of it or clearly behind it, never half-overlapping in a way that looks wrong.
- Lay scenery out so lanes and props do not fight: if a gag's lane would cross a prop awkwardly, move the prop or the lane rather than accept the overlap.
- Example (surveyor): he walks a lane behind the cow and the bushes, or the cow and bushes sit where his path never crosses them.
- Every gag gets a depth check in its filmstrip on all five regions' standard scenes.

## 2. Same start, same end
The last frame of every gag matches the first: the empty standard scene. Characters enter and exit fully off screen; nothing appears or vanishes by magic.

## 3. Standard scene
Every gag in a region uses that region's one standard background. Gags never add or remove scenery. Any prop a gag uses is permanent scenery.

## 4. Side profile
Every strip character is drawn in true side profile (one eye on the side of the head). No front-facing heads that read as a cyclops.

## 5. Size
A character never changes size between poses or drawings.

## 6. Acting
Characters face the way they travel (hop-turn first), anticipation before big moves, squash and stretch, arcs, ease in and out, follow-through, a small payoff.

## 7. Language
Finds are "sightings", never "Easter eggs" (the dinosaur egg is just an egg).

## 8. Content
No new bathroom humour (existing gags stay). Accurate Alberta oilfield terms and equipment. Accurate geology (one real west-central Alberta column near Fox Creek).

## 9. Triggers
No time-based gag triggers. Only night dimming is idle-based.

## 10. Checks
Visual fixes are checked in WebKit at iPhone DPR 3 plus Galaxy and Pixel sizes, not only Chromium.

## 11. Slow Moe, the anti-hero (Jay, Oct 8)
Any time a worker gets into trouble or a compromised situation, that worker is Slow Moe: orange FR coveralls with reflective stripes, stubble (no beard), droopy lids, hard hat tipped back, same build and size as every worker. He is the anti-hero who gets into trouble all the time. The bearded worker in red is the lucky, competent one. This applies to every region, old gags included.

## 12. Vehicles in the strip (Jay, Oct 7)
Trucks face right in the strip, so the driver door is on the far side. Drivers get in and out out of sight (clunk, small cab rock) and walk round the front. No near door ever opens. Also check Safari's visible viewport (about 390x664 and 375x635), not only full-screen.
